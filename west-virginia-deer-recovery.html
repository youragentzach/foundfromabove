Drone deer recovery in West Virginia

West Virginia wrote its drone recovery rule into the code, and wrote it strict. Under West Virginia Code section 20-2-5j, a legally hunting person who reasonably believes they mortally wounded a deer, elk, turkey, wild boar, or bear may use a UAV, personally or through a licensed UAV operator, to track and locate it.

The conditions are the strictest among the states that allow recovery: tracking by UAV is legally hunting, so a valid hunting license is required; the hunter must accompany the handler; paid operators must be licensed outfitters or guides; and the operator may never dispatch the animal or direct its dispatch. Written into the code, and our sources

West Virginia did not leave drone recovery to enforcement discretion. Section 20-2-5j of the code sets the permission and its conditions in statute: who may track, which animals, the license layers, the hunter's presence, and the hard line that the operator never dispatches or directs a dispatch.

Because the statute layers three different licenses, we verify West Virginia operators against all of them: hunting license for the hunter, outfitter or guide licensing for paid operators, and Part 107 for operators working off their own or leased land.

Questions

West Virginia deer recovery FAQ

Is drone deer recovery legal in West Virginia?

Yes, under West Virginia Code section 20-2-5j. A legally hunting person who reasonably believes they mortally wounded a deer, elk, turkey, wild boar, or bear may use a UAV, personally or through a licensed UAV operator, to track and locate it. The conditions are the strictest of any state that allows it.

Who has to be there during the flight?

The hunter. Tracking by UAV is legally hunting in West Virginia, and the hunter must accompany the handler during the search. A pilot does not fly a West Virginia recovery alone.

What licenses apply in West Virginia?

Three layers. The hunter needs a valid hunting license, because UAV tracking is legally hunting. A for-profit operator must be a licensed outfitter or guide. And an operator working off their own or leased property needs an FAA Part 107 certificate.

Can the pilot finish the animal?

Never. The UAV operator may not dispatch the animal, and may not direct its dispatch either. The operator's job ends at locating; everything after that is the hunter's, handled lawfully on the ground.

Which animals does the West Virginia rule cover?

Deer, elk, turkey, wild boar, and bear, where the hunter reasonably believes the animal was mortally wounded.

What does drone deer recovery cost in West Virginia?

Most searches run $350 to $450. Your pilot confirms the full price before you book, and the $100 deposit is charged only after a pilot accepts your request.
