A note on deer recovery and the law

Drone deer recovery rules vary a lot by state. Some states allow it with conditions, some prohibit it, and some have not settled the question.

We offer deer recovery only in the states listed above, under each state's conditions. In states where the law is unclear, we do not sell deer searches until the wildlife agency confirms the position in writing.

Georgia: deer recovery by drone is offered in Georgia, under the conditions Georgia DNR Law Enforcement confirmed to us in writing in October 2026. Those conditions: private property only, only to locate lawfully hunted wounded or downed game, landowner permission before takeoff or landing, no flying on DNR, USFS, USACOE, or other DNR managed property, the operator is never the taker, and no scouting, pursuit, or harassment of wildlife. Georgia pilots on this network also take pet and livestock work. Full details are on our Georgia deer recovery page.

If you are not sure about your state, ask us in the request form and we will give you a straight answer.

Common questions

Straight answers

What does a search cost?

Guide pricing: deer recovery typically runs $350 to $450 per search, pet searches run $200 to $450 with a standard search around $300, and livestock searches run about $300 per 2 hour search. Acreage, terrain, and travel can move the price, and some pilots charge a success fee if the animal is found. Your pilot confirms the full price with you before anything is booked, so there are no surprises.

How fast can a pilot get to me?

It depends on pilot availability near you and on daylight, weather, and airspace. Many searches can be flown the same day or the same night, and thermal works especially well after dark and in the early morning. Send the request as early as you can: for deer and pets, the first hours matter most.

What happens if the animal is not found?

You still get a full professional search: the property flown, heat signatures checked, and a report of what was covered. The search fee still applies even if the animal is not located, because the pilot's time, travel, and flight are the service. A success fee, where a pilot charges one, is only due when the animal is found.

Is drone deer recovery legal in my state?

It depends on the state, and the rules are changing fast. We currently offer deer recovery in Ohio, Missouri, Tennessee, Indiana, Wisconsin, Kentucky, Louisiana, Connecticut, Maryland, West Virginia, and Georgia, under each state's conditions. Georgia is offered under the conditions Georgia DNR Law Enforcement confirmed in writing in October 2026: private property only, lawfully hunted game only, landowner permission to take off or land, and no flying on DNR managed property. In states where the law is unclear we hold off until the wildlife agency confirms its position in writing. Pet and livestock searches do not carry the same restrictions and are available nationwide.

What do pilots need to join?

Three things: an FAA Part 107 Remote Pilot certificate, current insurance, and a thermal equipped drone. A track record of real recovery work helps you get placed faster. Founding Pilots list free for year one and pay a 10 percent platform booking fee only when a job books.

How do booking and deposits work?

Send the search request form and we match you with a local verified pilot. Once you agree on the time and the price, a deposit confirms the booking and holds your slot. The balance is settled under the terms your pilot confirms with you before the flight. A $100 deposit holds the booking once a pilot accepts, so your slot is confirmed before anyone drives out.
