Is drone deer recovery legal in North Carolina?

Short answer: it is unconfirmed, and the weight of what exists points toward no. North Carolina sits in the Unclear column of our state by state legality database, but this is the hardest kind of unclear: a statute that bans hunting with a drone, no published exception for recovery, and an enforcement consensus that treats recovery as part of hunting.

GS 14-401.24, as amended by S.L. 2023-69, makes it a Class 1 misdemeanor to fish or hunt using an unmanned aircraft system. We looked for a published recovery exception, the kind of written carve out that makes deer recovery lawful in states like Kentucky and Wisconsin, and did not find one. So Found From Above does not sell deer recovery in North Carolina. Lost pet search and livestock recovery are different: no deer legality gate applies to them, and both are offered here now. What would change this

What confirmation would need to say

Our position changes on written agency confirmation or a change in the law itself, the same standard that moved Georgia to Allowed in October 2026. Either of these would do it:

  • A written NCWRC reading of the statuteA direct written statement from the NC Wildlife Resources Commission that locating a wounded or dead deer after a legal hunt is not hunting under GS 14-401.24.
  • Or a statutory recovery exceptionAn amendment to the law that carves out post-shot recovery by drone, the way other states have written theirs.
  • The conditions stated with itWhose land it may be flown over, what permission is required, any season or hour limits, and whether the operator may tell the hunter the animal's location if it is still alive.

Until one of those exists, our answer stays no for deer and yes for pets and livestock. If you are a North Carolina hunter or pilot and you hold written guidance from NCWRC we have not seen, send it to us and we will update this page and credit the source.

North Carolina FAQ

Is drone deer recovery legal in North Carolina?

It is unconfirmed, and likely prohibited in practice. GS 14-401.24, as amended by S.L. 2023-69, makes it a Class 1 misdemeanor to fish or hunt using an unmanned aircraft system, and we found no published recovery exception. The forum consensus is that the NC Wildlife Resources Commission treats drones as banned for any part of hunting, including recovery. Whether NCWRC counts post-shot recovery as hunting is unresolved, and that question is decisive. Until it is answered in writing, we treat North Carolina deer recovery as not offered.

Why do some lists say North Carolina allows drone recovery?

A national deer association survey lists North Carolina as recovery allowed. That is a survey interpretation, and it sits against the statute text, which bans hunting with an unmanned aircraft system and names no recovery exception. A survey answer is not the agency and not the law, so it is not enough to sell a booking on.

Can I hire you to find a deer in North Carolina?

No, not yet. Found From Above does not sell deer recovery in North Carolina until NCWRC confirms in writing that post-shot recovery is not hunting under GS 14-401.24, or the statute gains a recovery exception. If either happens, this page changes and deer recovery opens here.

Can you search for a lost dog or livestock in North Carolina?

Yes. Pet and livestock search does not run through deer recovery law, and both are offered in North Carolina. Send a request and we will call back within about 30 minutes, 5 AM to 11 PM ET. Overnight requests get a call by 9 AM ET.

What should a North Carolina hunter do meanwhile?

Ground tracking first: mark last blood, back out, and work the trail on foot or with a tracking dog where legal. If you want the drone question answered for your situation, ask the NC Wildlife Resources Commission directly. A written answer from the agency is what changes our position, and we would rather wait for it than guess with your hunt.
