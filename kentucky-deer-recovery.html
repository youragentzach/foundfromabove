Kentucky drone deer recovery: deceased animals only

Kentucky's rule is a narrow carve-out inside a broad ban. Regulation 301 KAR 3:140 (effective 2025) prohibits using an aircraft or UAS to hunt or take wildlife, or to drive, herd, or harass it. Within that ban, the Kentucky Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources position is that a drone operator may notify a hunter of the location of a deceased animal. Locating live game for pursuit remains banned, and giving a hunter coordinates for a live wounded animal is prohibited.

We run Kentucky deliberately narrow, and one caveat belongs up front: warden interpretation of the live wounded animal line can vary. Every Kentucky booking on this network is handled as deceased animals only, with the stop rule below applied without exception. The Kentucky rule

How recovery works in Kentucky

  • Deceased animals onlyA Kentucky recovery flight looks for an animal that is already dead. That is the whole of the carve-out, and we do not stretch it.
  • Notify, do not guideThe operator may tell the hunter the location of a deceased animal. Guiding a hunter toward a live animal, or sharing coordinates for one, is prohibited.
  • No driving, herding, or harassing301 KAR 3:140 bans using an aircraft or UAS to hunt, take, drive, herd, or harass wildlife. None of that is part of a recovery flight.
  • If it is alive, the flight endsIf the animal is found alive, the flight stops and no location that would help a hunt is shared. A licensed tracking dog is the lawful next step for a live wounded deer in Kentucky.
  • Private land, with permissionThe operator needs the landowner's permission for the ground they take off from, land on, and work over.
  • FAA rules applyCommercial pilots on this network hold an FAA Part 107 certificate and current insurance, and every pilot is verified before a listing goes live.

Why we say allowed with limits, and our sources

Kentucky did not create a drone recovery program. It kept a broad ban on aircraft and UAS for hunting and taking wildlife (301 KAR 3:140), and the Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources reads that ban to permit one thing: an operator notifying a hunter of the location of a deceased animal. Forum claims that Kentucky specifically made drone recovery legal overstate it; it is a carve-out inside a ban.

Because warden interpretation varies, especially where a wounded animal is still alive, we treat any live animal as outside the service entirely. If your deer may still be alive and mobile, we will tell you plainly and point you to a tracking dog instead.

Questions

Kentucky deer recovery FAQ

Is drone deer recovery legal in Kentucky?

In a narrow form, yes. Under 301 KAR 3:140, aircraft and UAS may not be used to hunt, take, drive, herd, or harass wildlife, but the Kentucky Department of Fish and Wildlife Resources position permits a drone operator to notify a hunter of the location of a deceased animal. Locating live game remains banned.

Can a pilot guide me to a live wounded deer in Kentucky?

No. Giving a hunter the location of a live wounded animal for pursuit is prohibited. If a search finds the animal alive, the flight ends and no coordinates are shared. A licensed tracking dog is the lawful option for a live wounded deer.

What if the drone finds my deer alive?

The flight stops. The pilot will tell you the deer was located alive in general terms only where that does not amount to guiding a hunt, and the honest next step is a tracking dog and a fresh plan on the ground. No location for a live animal is ever passed through this network.

What does drone deer recovery cost in Kentucky?

Most Kentucky searches run about $350 to $450. Some pilots add a success fee, often around $100, charged only when your deer is found. Your pilot confirms the full price before you book, and a $100 deposit holds the booking once a pilot accepts.

What should I do before the pilot arrives?

Mark the last blood and the direction of travel, then back out and stay out of the area. Note the time of the shot and what the sign says about the hit, and have landowner permission ready for the ground the pilot launches from.

What if the drone does not find my deer?

The search fee still applies, because the service is the pilot's time, travel, and flight. A thermal search improves the odds a great deal, but no honest operator guarantees a recovery.
