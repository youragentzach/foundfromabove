The DNR conditions

How recovery works in Georgia

  • Legally hunted game onlyThe drone may only locate wounded or downed game that the hunter legally hunted. The flight happens after the shot, never before.
  • Private property onlyRecovery flights happen on private property. Public land is out, as the next condition explains.
  • Landowner permission firstThe operator must have the landowner's permission to take off or land on the property before flying.
  • No DNR managed propertyDrones are not permitted for recovery on DNR, USFS, USACOE, or any other property managed by DNR. That includes WMAs.
  • The operator is not the takerPer DNR, the drone operator is not considered to be hunting, provided they are not the person who took the game. The hunter retrieves and tags the deer on foot.
  • Recovery only, never scoutingA drone may not be used to aid in the pursuit, scouting, harassing, or taking of any wildlife. Locating lawfully hunted wounded or downed game is the only permitted use.
  • Part 107 pilots, FAA rulesAll FAA regulations apply, and DNR identifies the FAA Part 107 certificate as the correct certificate. Every pilot we book holds Part 107, carries current insurance, and flies a thermal equipped drone.

Why we say legal, and our source

Before offering deer recovery in Georgia, we wrote to the Georgia Department of Natural Resources Law Enforcement Division and asked how the law applies to post-harvest drone recovery. On October 19, 2026, Lt. Adam Loudermilk of the Law Enforcement Division replied in writing: drones are permitted to be used to locate downed or wounded game, on the conditions listed on this page.

One honest nuance: this is the Division's written enforcement position, not a change to Georgia's statute. The text of O.C.G.A. section 27-3-13 has not changed, and we do not claim otherwise. We operate to the Division's stated conditions, and if that position changes, we will update this page and our bookings to match.

This page is general information, not legal advice.

Source: Georgia DNR Law Enforcement Division, written reply to Found From Above, October 19, 2026.

Questions

Georgia deer recovery FAQ

Is drone deer recovery legal in Georgia?

Yes, with conditions. Georgia DNR Law Enforcement confirmed in writing in October 2026 that drones are permitted to locate downed or wounded game in Georgia: private property only, only for game the hunter legally hunted, landowner permission to take off or land, never on DNR, USFS, USACOE, or other DNR managed property, and never to scout, pursue, harass, or aid in taking wildlife. This is the agency's written enforcement position, not a change to the statute.

What are the conditions for drone deer recovery in Georgia?

Per Georgia DNR Law Enforcement: the deer must have been legally hunted; the flight is on private property only; the operator needs landowner permission to take off or land; recovery is not permitted on DNR, USFS, USACOE, or other DNR managed property; the drone operator is not the taker of the game; the drone may not aid pursuit, scouting, harassing, or taking; and all FAA rules apply, with the Part 107 certificate as the correct certificate.

What does drone deer recovery cost in Georgia?

Most Georgia searches run about $350 to $450. Some pilots add a success fee, often around $100, charged only when your deer is found. Your pilot confirms the full price before you book, and a $100 deposit holds the booking once a pilot accepts.

Can a drone recover my deer on a WMA or other public land in Georgia?

No. Georgia DNR Law Enforcement's position is that drone recovery is not permitted on DNR, USFS, USACOE, or other DNR managed property. Georgia recovery flights on this network are private property only, with landowner permission.

What should I do before the pilot arrives?

Mark the last blood and the direction of travel, then back out and stay out of the area. Grid walking can push a wounded deer and spreads scent everywhere. Note the time of the shot, and have landowner permission ready for the ground the pilot launches from and any ground you may need to cross.

What if the drone does not find my deer?

The search fee still applies, because the service is the pilot's time, travel, and flight. A thermal search improves the odds a great deal, but no honest operator guarantees a recovery.
