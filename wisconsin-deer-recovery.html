Wisconsin drone deer recovery: thermal to locate, spotlight to confirm

Wisconsin's answer comes from the Wisconsin DNR's own Law Enforcement Handbook (750 LEH, January 2024): it is acceptable for a UAS operator to use thermal imaging plus mounted spotlights to locate and confirm a killed or reasonably presumed dead animal. The same guidance draws the bright line on the other side: using thermal imaging or a drone to locate a live animal while hunting violates state law (s. 29.307). So the Wisconsin method is a two step discipline: thermal finds the candidate, the spotlight confirms it is dead, and only then does anyone walk in.

One honest note on the source: this is DNR enforcement guidance published in its law enforcement handbook, not a statutory carve-out, so we state it exactly that way and fly to the guidance as written. The Wisconsin guidance

How recovery works in Wisconsin

  • Thermal to locateThe pilot flies a thermal grid over the property to find candidate heat signatures, starting from last blood and the direction of travel.
  • Spotlight to confirmA mounted spotlight confirms the animal is killed or reasonably presumed dead before any location is shared or anyone walks in. No confirmation, no walk-in.
  • Never locate live gameUsing thermal imaging or a UAS to locate a live animal while hunting violates s. 29.307. Recovery of a downed animal is the only permitted use, and scouting is never part of it.
  • If it is alive, stopIf the animal turns out to be alive and mobile, it is not a recoverable animal under this guidance and the flight ends.
  • Private land, with permissionThe operator needs the landowner's permission for the ground they take off from, land on, and work over.
  • FAA rules applyCommercial pilots on this network hold an FAA Part 107 certificate and current insurance, and every pilot is verified before a listing goes live.

Why we say allowed, and our sources

Wisconsin has no drone recovery statute. What it has is the DNR's Law Enforcement Handbook, the manual its own wardens work from, which states that a UAS operator may use thermal imaging plus mounted spotlights to locate and confirm a killed or reasonably presumed dead animal, and that using the same tools to locate a live animal while hunting violates s. 29.307.

Because the source is enforcement guidance rather than a statute, field interpretation could vary. We operate to the guidance exactly as written: locate with thermal, confirm dead with the spotlight, and never use the aircraft on live game.

This page is general information, not legal advice.

Sources: Wisconsin DNR Law Enforcement Handbook (750 LEH, January 2024); Wis. Stat. s. 29.307.

Questions

Wisconsin deer recovery FAQ

Is drone deer recovery legal in Wisconsin?

Wisconsin DNR's Law Enforcement Handbook (750 LEH, January 2024) states it is acceptable for a UAS operator to use thermal imaging plus mounted spotlights to locate and confirm a killed or reasonably presumed dead animal. It is enforcement guidance, not a statute, and using thermal or a drone to locate a live animal while hunting violates s. 29.307.

What is the thermal plus spotlight rule?

Thermal imaging locates a candidate heat signature. A mounted spotlight then confirms the animal is killed or reasonably presumed dead. Only after that confirmation is a location shared and anyone walks in. If the animal is alive and mobile, the flight ends.

Can a drone be used to find a live deer for hunting in Wisconsin?

No. Using thermal imaging or a UAS to locate a live animal while hunting violates state law (s. 29.307). Our pilots fly recovery only, after a legal shot, and never scout or locate live game.

What does drone deer recovery cost in Wisconsin?

Most Wisconsin searches run about $350 to $450. Some pilots add a success fee, often around $100, charged only when your deer is found. Your pilot confirms the full price before you book, and a $100 deposit holds the booking once a pilot accepts.

What should I do before the pilot arrives?

Mark the last blood and the direction of travel, then back out and stay out of the area. Grid walking can push a wounded deer and spread scent. Note the time of the shot, and have landowner permission ready for the ground the pilot launches from.

What if the drone does not find my deer?

The search fee still applies, because the service is the pilot's time, travel, and flight. A thermal search improves the odds a great deal, but no honest operator guarantees a recovery.
