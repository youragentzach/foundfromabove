Tennessee

Tennessee drone deer recovery is legal. Here is how the rule works.

Tennessee has moved from banning drone deer recovery to regulating it. The General Assembly authorized drones solely for locating and recovering wounded deer (Public Chapter 478, from House Bill 175 and Senate Bill 130), the Tennessee Fish and Wildlife Commission approved TWRA's implementing rule effective August 1, 2026, and on October 22, 2026 the rule received a positive recommendation in committee review. As of October 13, 40 commercial operators were already on TWRA's registration list. Found From Above is building its Tennessee roster of verified thermal pilots now.

The TWRA rule

How recovery works in Tennessee

  • Recovery onlyThe deer must be reasonably believed to be mortally wounded or dead, and traditional tracking must have failed first. Locating a lawfully hunted wounded deer is the only permitted use.
  • Private land only, with permissionRecovery flights happen on private land, and the operator needs the landowner's permission for the ground they work from.
  • Night flightsRecovery flights run at night, beginning 30 minutes after sunset, when thermal imaging separates a downed deer from the woods around it.
  • FAA certified pilots, thermal plus lightThe pilot must hold a current FAA remote pilot certificate and use thermal imaging plus a light source to verify the animal's status before anyone walks in.
  • No hunting with the droneNo pursuing, harassing, or driving wildlife, no weapons carried during an operation, and no herd surveys or census flights during open deer seasons.
  • Commercial operators registerCommercial pilots register with TWRA one time at no fee and file pre and post recovery reports for commercial jobs. A hunter flying their own drone follows the same recovery rules without the commercial reports.

Why we say legal, and our sources

Tennessee's path was statute first, rule second. The General Assembly passed the authorizing law in 2025 (Public Chapter 478), directing the Commission to set the rules for drone recovery of wounded deer. TWRA built its rule with a work group of wildlife officers, drone pilots, and hunters, the Commission approved it in January 2026, and it took effect August 1, 2026. In October 2026 the rule cleared a positive committee recommendation in legislative review, where members pressed on enforceability and fair chase.

One honest nuance: the rule is young, and TWRA can refine it. We operate to the rule as published, and if TWRA updates it, we will update this page and our bookings to match.

This page is general information, not legal advice.

Sources: Tennessee Public Chapter 478 (HB 175 / SB 130); TWRA rule proceedings, Tennessee Fish and Wildlife Commission, January and October 2026.

How it works

From lost trail to recovered deer

1

Track first, then call

Tennessee's rule starts with traditional tracking. When the trail is lost, mark the last blood, note the time of the shot and the direction of travel, then back out and send the request. The first evening is the best window.

2

Match and confirm

We match a verified, TWRA registered Tennessee pilot near you and confirm the landowner permission for the launch spot. The pilot confirms the full price and an arrival window, and a $100 deposit holds the booking.

3

Fly and recover

After dark, the pilot flies a thermal grid over the property, verifies the deer with thermal and light, and guides you in for the recovery. A success fee applies only if your pilot charges one and the deer is found.

Questions

Tennessee deer recovery FAQ

Is drone deer recovery legal in Tennessee?

Yes. Tennessee law (Public Chapter 478) authorizes drones solely to locate and recover wounded deer, and TWRA's implementing rule took effect August 1, 2026. Recovery is private land only with landowner permission, at night beginning 30 minutes after sunset, by a pilot holding a current FAA remote pilot certificate using thermal imaging. Commercial operators must register with TWRA and file pre and post recovery reports.

What are the rules for drone deer recovery in Tennessee?

Per TWRA's rule: private land only with landowner permission; the deer must be reasonably believed mortally wounded or dead and traditional tracking must have failed; flights begin 30 minutes after sunset; the pilot holds a current FAA remote pilot certificate and uses thermal imaging plus a light source; no weapons during the operation; and drones may not be used to hunt, pursue, harass, or survey herds during open deer seasons. Commercial operators register with TWRA once at no fee and file pre and post recovery reports.

What does drone deer recovery cost in Tennessee?

Most Tennessee searches run about $350 to $450. Some pilots add a success fee, often around $100, charged only when your deer is found. Your pilot confirms the full price before you book, and a $100 deposit holds the booking once a pilot accepts.

Do Tennessee drone recovery pilots have to register?

Commercial operators do: TWRA registration is one time and free, and commercial jobs require pre and post recovery reports filed with TWRA. As of October 13, 2026, 40 commercial operators were already on TWRA's list. A hunter using their own drone follows the same recovery rules but does not file the commercial reports.

What should I do before the pilot arrives?

Mark the last blood and the direction of travel, then back out and stay out of the area. Tennessee's rule expects traditional tracking to have been tried first, and grid walking after that can push a wounded deer and spread scent. Note the time of the shot, and have landowner permission ready for the ground the pilot launches from.

What if the drone does not find my deer?

The search fee still applies, because the service is the pilot's time, travel, and flight. A thermal search improves the odds a great deal, but no honest operator guarantees a recovery.
